# R87: the 'submeter' workload holds one pid

**What changed.** `src/submeter/attribution/rules.py`: `Attributor` takes `self_pids`, a collection of pids, in place of `self_pid`. Every pid in it is the `submeter` workload, and children inherit as they did before. `src/submeter/cli.py` (`top`) passes `{os.getpid()}`. The server and launcher holders add their pids once they exist (I45, whose mechanism the owner has still to choose). The budget sentences in docs/08, docs/15 and docs/14 are the docs worker part.

**Test.** `tests/test_attribution.py::test_every_submeter_pid_and_its_children_are_submeter`: `Attributor(RuleSet(), self_pids={10, 20})` puts pids 10 and 20 and a child of 20 in `submeter`, and leaves an unrelated process unattributed.

**Note.** The set holds pids alone, as `self_pid` did and as the plan states. AGENTS.md identifies a process by pid plus start time; I45's assumption matches Submeter's processes on both, which belongs with the holders' pid files when they are built.

**Follow-up: a pid registered late.** The checker found that after R47 the sentence "the server and launcher holders add their pids once they exist" could not be kept. `self_pids` was fixed when the `Attributor` was built, and a new `Attributor` discards R47's map. A Submeter pid that the collector had already seen also kept its remembered workload, because `_remembered` let only a kernel group override it. Holders start while the collector runs, so the collector will usually see a holder before it learns the holder's pid. The holder would then stay in its launcher's workload, and Submeter would under-report its own cost again. `Attributor.set_self_pids()` now replaces the set from the next `assign()`. In `_remembered`, a Submeter pid overrides a remembered workload as a kernel group does, and the move goes into `changes`. The module docstring says so.
