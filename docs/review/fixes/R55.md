# R55. A wall-clock step back reopens a closed day file, packs 90 covered seconds into a minute and delays keyframes; a step forward discards an observed interval

**What changed.** `src/submeter/collector/sampler.py`: `taken_at` is the previous `taken_at` plus the growth of the interval clock (`Snapshot.monotonic`, which counts suspend under I92), so a suspend does not make it lag. When the wall clock runs ahead of that by more than the slack and R59's suspend test does not fire, `taken_at` re-anchors to the wall clock. It never runs back: after a backward step it keeps counting on the interval clock and rejoins the wall clock when the wall clock catches up. The step is recorded once, in the new `SampleBatch.clock_step_s` (default 0.0; `src/submeter/core/models.py`), as the change in the wall clock's lead over `taken_at`, instead of marking a gap. `src/submeter/store/raw.py` `RawStore.write` (line 266): the day never goes earlier than the open file's day. The collector loop that writes the step's event row through `RawFile.record_event` (I41) is not built yet.

**Assumption.** The plan says both "re-anchor" and "taken_at never decreases". A backward step cannot do both, so it re-anchors forward only; recording the step as a change in the lead keeps a persistent backward offset from being reported on every tick.

**Tests.** `tests/test_collector.py::test_taken_at_follows_the_interval_clock_and_never_runs_back`: a 100 s forward step with both clocks agreeing gives a non-gap batch with a 1 s interval, `taken_at` 1101 and a recorded step of 100; a 60 s suspend leaves `taken_at` equal to the wall clock; a 30 s backward step gives a `taken_at` later than the previous tick and a recorded step of -30. `::test_a_raw_store_never_reopens_an_earlier_day`: a `RawStore` that opened 2026-11-20 and is then given a batch stamped one second before midnight writes it to the open file.
